# Find stakeholders

**Find, then add.** Search, find and research tools do not add prospect records, so you can run a search again as often as needed. Iterate until the set is right, and add only what the user approved. Adding (`trayo_import_accounts` for companies, `trayo_add_people` for people) is how you save them.

Collection results may use `delivery: "file"`. In that case, `preview` and `metadata` are compact and may be shortened; download `file.downloadUrl` and process the complete JSON in code before selecting or importing rows. If the download is blocked, as in Claude Cowork, read the same result with `trayo_read_result { resultId }` and follow `page.nextCursor` while `page.hasMore` is true; this never repeats the search. Keep the original `hasMore`/`nextCursor` pagination, and do not repeat the search to get its file. Use `output: "file"` when a download is wanted, or `output: "pages"` when you cannot download files.

1. `trayo_whoami` once.
2. For a stakeholder definition that needs interpretation, use `trayo_search_stakeholders` `{ company: { website | linkedinUrl | name } or { accountId }, definition, limit }` for each company. `definition` is the user's description of the roles that own, buy, influence and should be skipped; omit it to use the workspace's own, and read `definition.source` to see which applied — `source: "default"` means neither existed and the search fell back to seniority alone, which is worth fixing once with `trayo_set_workspace` rather than restating the roles on every call. 5 people per company by default, each with `whyThisPerson`; pass `limit` for more, up to 10. For explicit title and employer criteria, use direct search in step 3.
3. Call `trayo_find_people` with `mode: "direct"`, `filters` and no `query`. Use `filters.title.any` (with `all` and `exclude` for additional requirements), plus EITHER `filters.companyWebsites` (up to 100 company websites) OR employer filters such as `industries`, `hq` and `headcount`. These company selectors are mutually exclusive. Use `trayo_list_industries` for accepted industry values. Company criteria match the current employer; title criteria match the current position at that employer. `hq` is company headquarters, not the person's own location. A direct people search needs a title (`any` or `all`) plus named companies or an indexed company filter: `industries`, `hq.cities`, `hq.states` or `headcount`. `find_needs_indexed_filter` means that company filter is missing, not that nobody matched. When the user names no company, industry, city, state or size, as in "find people with the title Marketing Manager", do not ask for one: choose the company filters as described in "Company filters the user did not give" below, and keep every filter the user did give.
4. Expand equivalent title spellings yourself: `title.any: ["cto", "chief technology officer"]` matches either spelling. Preserve independent requirements, numeric bounds, geography and exclusions; state which requested conditions cannot be checked. `perCompany` defaults to 4 and is at most 25. A cap note with `truncated: true` means more matching people may exist; following a cursor does not remove that per-company limit. A thin answer carries `next` guidance. Keep direct mode when refining; after changing filters, sort or `perCompany`, restart without the old cursor. The existing sentence search remains available without direct mode when semantic interpretation is needed; do not switch to it silently because a direct result is thin or failed.
5. Present the people per company with their reasons. Compare the person's title at the searched company with `otherCurrentRoles` (company and title) before presenting them as a buyer. `otherCurrentEmployers` lists other companies with roles, including affiliations; it does not establish who employs or pays someone. A CTO here with an advisory seat elsewhere can still be a buyer here. A jury member here with an operating role elsewhere is an affiliate, and should not be presented as staff or assigned buying authority without evidence. Missing context or an empty list proves neither employment nor non-employment; state uncertainty when the roles do not settle it. A person whose employer is "Self Employed" or a similar placeholder works independently; do not present them as staff of a company. No new people have been added by this search, so this is where to iterate. Let the user sharpen the `definition`, the title terms, `perCompany` or the company set, and search again as often as it takes. Move on only when they have picked the people to keep.
6. Put the picks in the workspace with `trayo_add_people`, up to 200 rows. `trayo_find_people` rows can be passed as they came back. `trayo_search_stakeholders` rows carry no company, so add the one you searched: `{ fullName, title, linkedinUrl, accountId }` when the company is already an account, or `{ fullName, title, linkedinUrl, company: { name, domain } }` to link one by name. `domain` gives the strongest company identity; when it is unavailable, `{ name }` still creates a name-only account. `fullName` is the only required field and the professional profile URL is the upsert key, so send it whenever the row carries one. Read ids from both `created` and `updated`; an `updated` row matched an existing person by LinkedIn or email and wrote the fields you sent. A `skipped` row with `reasonCode: "duplicate"` is a name-only match that was deliberately left unchanged; use its `existingId` only after confirming it is the same human. `reasonCode: "identity_conflict"` means LinkedIn and email identify different people; send the row again with only the identity that belongs to the person you mean. Then read `degraded`, which names rows that DID land but landed incomplete: `no_account` is a row with no account, `no_identity` a row with neither a LinkedIn nor an email. A `no_identity` row linked to an account is replay-safe by `fullName`; an unlinked one has no deduplication key and a replay creates it again. Say so rather than reporting a clean import.
7. Make them reachable before you hand anything over. First tell the user the workspace's lookup allowance: `lookups.email.remaining` from `trayo_whoami` (`null` means no plan cap). Every lookup that starts draws on it, so send only the people the user picked. `trayo_enrich_emails { personIds }`, up to 200 ids per call, starts the lookups and waits; `contacts` holds what landed, and `trayo_get_contacts { personIds }` reads anything still running for free, no faster than the wait `next` offers. A person whose address is already known or already being looked up is skipped at no cost. Use `email.value`, and flag an `email.identityConfidence` of `conflict` for a human look. When `stoppedBy` comes back, the allowance ran out part-way: say which `reason` it names and stop rather than loop. Skill `enrich-contacts` has the same loop in more detail, including phone numbers.

Stakeholder search answers in 5–20 s per company. For several companies, send them in one `trayo_search_stakeholders_batch` call (up to 50 companies under one `definition`) and read it with `trayo_get_stakeholder_search` until it finishes, instead of calling `trayo_search_stakeholders` once per company. Report progress. `company_not_found` is `do_not_retry` — the company could not be resolved from what you sent, so send its website or company profile URL instead.

Busy, unavailable and timeout errors are failed searches, never evidence that nobody matched. Follow the returned retry guidance and delay, keep calls sequential, and report persistent failure. Do not drop filters or change mode to bypass a limit. Read notes and truncation before claiming that all matching people were found.

## Company filters the user did not give

This applies only to a filters-only search (`mode: "direct"`) that names no company, industry, city, state or size, as in "find people with the title Marketing Manager". A sentence search with `query` needs no added company filter. Do not ask the user. Choose the company filters this way, and keep every filter the user did give:

1. Read `trayo_get_workspace`.
2. Industries: when `buyerProfile.industries` is not empty and `buyerProfileSource` is `saved` or `partial`, map each one to an exact value from `trayo_list_industries` and send the mapped values as `filters.industries`. A value outside that list matches no company, and no note says so. For example, "Data Infrastructure & Analytics" becomes "data infrastructure and analytics", and "Computer Software" is already covered by "software development". Tell the user which profile industries did not map.
3. Size band: with a `saved` profile whose industries mapped in step 2, send `buyerProfile.sizeBand` as `filters.headcount`, lowering its `max` to 50,000 when it is higher; say so when you lower it. In every other case (a `partial` profile, no profile industry mapped, or no profile), send `filters.headcount: { min: 1, max: 10000 }`. Never send `{ min: 1 }` alone: the largest employers come first, and a common title can time out there.
4. A country as the only place: a country cannot select companies on its own. With mapped profile industries, send the country as `hq.countries` together with those industries and the band from step 3. Without them, pin the country's main cities (for example `"tallinn||EE"`) in `hq.cities`, or its states in `hq.states`, instead of the country, and keep the band. A city pin is also the fastest of these searches. Say which places you chose.
5. Only a funding stage, founded year, technology or company type: these narrow a set but do not select one. Add the profile industries from step 2 and a band that fits the request, such as `{ min: 1, max: 50 }` for seed-stage companies. They are checked company by company, so read `residual_scan_limit_reached`. With that note or `filter_values_truncated`, an empty or short page is not proof of no matches, even when `hasMore` is false: send one industry per request, or add cities, and merge the pages.

Then tell the user which company filters you chose and where they came from: the workspace profile, or companies with at most 10,000 people. Offer to narrow or widen them by industry, place, size or named companies. Never say every company was searched. A direct title search checks at most the first 30 companies in `sort` order: the largest first by default, or the smallest with `sort: "smallest_first"`. `company_fan_out_limit_reached` means more companies matched than were searched. `residual_scan_limit_reached` means only the first selected companies in `sort` order were checked (the largest, by default). `title_matched_first` means the title was rare, so pages run in company-id order, not size order, and paging until `hasMore` is false returns every match, at most `perCompany` per company.

## Scale an approved people preview

For a direct-search total such as 1,000, keep the preview's full results, then follow its search `nextCursor` with `mode: "direct"` and at most 100 records per call. Keep filters, sort and `perCompany` unchanged; request no more than the remaining count. Read each full file or saved-result pages, combine and deduplicate, and stop at the requested count or when `hasMore` is false. Report truncation and any shortfall. `output: "file"` or `"pages"` changes delivery, not the search limit. Never send `limit: 1000` in direct mode or remove the mode to increase the page size.

An existing filters-only preview made without direct mode can still use `limit: 1000`, the same filters, sort and `perCompany`, `output: "file"` (or `"pages"`), and no cursor for a total including the preview. Check `rowCount`, `collection.stopReason` and `hasMore` before continuing with `nextCursor`. Sentence and single-company stakeholder searches keep their existing limits; preserve the approved meaning when choosing a different search.

## Finish

Hand back the people per company with their reasons and, once step 7 ran, their addresses. Say how many still have no address, and why.

By now you must have: presented the people and let the user iterate before adding anyone; added only their picks; stated the workspace's lookup allowance before spending it.

Offer these in one line, then wait for the user's pick:
- Keep it in Trayo: `trayo_add_to_list` `{ name, members: [{ personId, via: 'find' }] }` — the working set the app shows under your user.
- Run it again on request: save the `definition` with `trayo_set_workspace`, then run the same `trayo_search_stakeholders` or `trayo_find_people` call when the user asks. To script it over the REST API instead, recipe `stakeholders-at-a-company` at `https://api.trayo.ai/v1/recipes` has the same steps.
- Hand it off: a CSV of name, title, company and email, written from the rows you hold.
