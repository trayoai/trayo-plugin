# Find stakeholders

**Find, then add.** Search, find and research tools save nothing, so you can run a search again as often as needed. Iterate until the set is right, and add only what the user approved. Adding (`trayo_import_accounts` for companies, `trayo_add_people` for people) is how you save them.

Collection results may use `delivery: "file"`. In that case, `preview` and `metadata` are compact and may be shortened; download `file.downloadUrl` and process the complete JSON in code before selecting or importing rows. If the download is blocked, as in Claude Cowork, read the same result with `trayo_read_result { resultId }` and follow `page.nextCursor` while `page.hasMore` is true; this never repeats the search. Keep the original `hasMore`/`nextCursor` pagination, and do not repeat the search to get its file. Use `output: "file"` when a download is wanted, or `output: "pages"` when you cannot download files.

1. `trayo_whoami` once.
2. For each company, `trayo_search_stakeholders` `{ company: { website | linkedinUrl | name } or { accountId }, definition, limit }` — `definition` is the user's description of the roles that own, buy, influence and should be skipped; omit it to use the workspace's own, and read `definition.source` to see which applied — `source: "default"` means neither existed and the search fell back to seniority alone, which is worth fixing once with `trayo_set_workspace` rather than restating the roles on every call. 5 people per company by default, each with `whyThisPerson`; pass `limit` for more, up to 10.
3. For many companies at once by title, `trayo_find_people` pages through Trayo's own data instead: `filters.title.any` (terms, with `all` and `exclude` to sharpen), plus EITHER `filters.companyWebsites` — the companies' own websites, up to 100, the same `website` a companies search returns — OR company filters such as `industries` and `hq`. The two are mutually exclusive, and with a `query` a people search may filter on `title` only. A filters-only people search needs a title (`any` or `all`) and one of those company filters: `companyWebsites`, or at least one indexed filter — `industries`, `hq.cities`, `hq.states` or `headcount`. Titles alone answer `find_needs_indexed_filter`, which means the filters cannot select a set, not that nobody matched.
4. Two things decide whether that search finds the people or misses them. Title terms are matched literally, so an acronym finds only the acronym — send both spellings (`["cto", "chief technology officer"]`). And a filters-only search reads at most `perCompany` people per company, most senior first: default 4, at most 25, so raise it when one company holds several of the people you want. A thin answer carries `next`, which names which of these to change; a `query` sentence instead of filters resolves the synonyms and the seniority for you and reports what it understood in `interpreted`.
5. Present the people per company with their reasons. Compare the person's title at the searched company with `otherCurrentRoles` (company and title) before presenting them as a buyer. `otherCurrentEmployers` lists other companies with roles, including affiliations; it does not establish who employs or pays someone. A CTO here with an advisory seat elsewhere can still be a buyer here. A jury member here with an operating role elsewhere is an affiliate, and should not be presented as staff or assigned buying authority without evidence. Missing context or an empty list proves neither employment nor non-employment; state uncertainty when the roles do not settle it. Nothing is saved yet, so this is where to iterate. Let the user sharpen the `definition`, the title terms, `perCompany` or the company set, and search again as often as it takes. Move on only when they have picked the people to keep.
6. Put the picks in the workspace with `trayo_add_people`, up to 200 rows. `trayo_find_people` rows can be passed as they came back. `trayo_search_stakeholders` rows carry no company, so add the one you searched: `{ fullName, title, linkedinUrl, accountId }` when the company is already an account, or `{ fullName, title, linkedinUrl, company: { name, domain } }` to link one by name. `domain` gives the strongest company identity; when it is unavailable, `{ name }` still creates a name-only account. `fullName` is the only required field and the professional profile URL is the upsert key, so send it whenever the row carries one. Read ids from both `created` and `updated`; an `updated` row matched an existing person by LinkedIn or email and wrote the fields you sent. A `skipped` row with `reasonCode: "duplicate"` is a name-only match that was deliberately left unchanged; use its `existingId` only after confirming it is the same human. `reasonCode: "identity_conflict"` means LinkedIn and email identify different people; send one identity, or PATCH the intended record. Then read `degraded`, which names rows that DID land but landed incomplete: `no_account` is a row with no account, `no_identity` a row with neither a LinkedIn nor an email. A `no_identity` row linked to an account is replay-safe by `fullName`; an unlinked one has no deduplication key and a replay creates it again. Say so rather than reporting a clean import.
7. Make them reachable before you hand anything over: `trayo_enrich_emails { personIds }` starts the lookups and waits, `contacts` holds what landed, and `trayo_get_contacts` reads anything still running for free. Skill `enrich-contacts` has the whole loop, including the workspace's lookup allowance and what to do when it runs out.

Stakeholder search answers in 5–20 s per company; run companies one at a time and report progress. `company_not_found` is `do_not_retry` — the company could not be resolved from what you sent, so send its website or company profile URL instead.

## Scale an approved people preview

For a total such as 1,000, call `trayo_find_people` again with the approved exact filters, sort and `perCompany`, `limit: 1000`, `output: "file"` (or `"pages"` when downloads are blocked), and no cursor. Preserve title criteria and the per-company cap. Download `file.downloadUrl` in code; check `rowCount`, `collection.stopReason` (file/inline) or first-page `metadata.collection.stopReason` (pages), and `hasMore`. Continue with `nextCursor` and the remaining count if needed. Sentence and single-company stakeholder searches keep their existing limits; first preview exact filters preserving the approved meaning.

## Finish

Hand back the people per company with their reasons and, once step 7 ran, their addresses. Say how many still have no address, and why.

By now you must have: presented the people and let the user iterate before adding anyone; added only their picks; stated the workspace's lookup allowance before spending it.

Offer these in one line, then wait for the user's pick:
- Keep it in Trayo: `trayo_add_to_list` `{ name, members: [{ personId, via: 'find' }] }` — the working set the app shows under your user.
- Re-run it on your cadence: recipe `stakeholders-at-a-company` at `https://api.trayo.ai/v1/recipes`, or `POST /v1/find` with the same `filters` across many companies.
- Hand it off: a CSV of name, title, company and email, written from the rows you hold.
